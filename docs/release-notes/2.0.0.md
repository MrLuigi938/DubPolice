# Dub Police 2.0 – The retro mode speaks too

<p align="center"><b>English</b> · <a href="2.0.0.de.md">Deutsch</a> · <a href="2.0.0.fr.md">Français</a></p>

Version 2.0 is the biggest update since Dub Police started. Until now, Dub Police brought the old voices into the **remasters**. Now the **original episodes of the retro mode**, which Steam installs with the remasters, get their dub back as well: Season 1 without choppy voices, Season 2 in German and French, Season 3 in German. And a badge on the main menu shows that everything is ready.

Season 3 took real detective work. The 2010 game uses a scene format of its own that is documented nowhere. Dub Police does not just swap the game for an old version there. It now understands the scenes down to the byte and adapts them to the German lines. More on that below.

## At a glance

- **New: Season 1 retro mode repaired** – the German and French original episodes that Steam ships no longer sound choppy.
- **New: Season 2 retro mode** – the original *Beyond Time and Space* episodes speak German or French, with scenes made to fit.
- **New: Season 3 retro mode** – the original *The Devil's Playhouse* episodes speak German, with subtitles including umlauts and scenes that wait for the longer lines.
- **New: Dub Police badge on the main menu** – a sticker on the game's logo says "Deutsch aktiv – viel Spaß!" (or "Français activé – bon jeu !"), so you see at once that everything is installed.
- **New: music in dialog pauses** – when a cutscene waits for a longer line, the music fades out and back in. A switch lets it keep running instead.
- **New: French mouth shapes** – in French, mouths now follow the French words too.
- **New: lips for gadgets and special characters** – gadgets such as the Moviefone or Sinistar, and characters with bone animations such as the Maimtron, the bugs or Sal, move in time with the new line.
- **Fixed:** nested scenes played in slow motion and were cut off at the end. Running retro episodes were not recognised as "game still running".

## The retro mode

The Season 2 and 3 remasters include the original 2008 to 2010 episodes as a retro mode of their own, but only in English. The window now has the checkbox **Retro-Version auch vertonen / Doubler aussi la version rétro**. Tick it, and the retro mode gets the voices and texts from your old version. Untick it, and it speaks English again. Your original files stay unchanged.

### Season 1: Save the World

- Steam already ships the original episodes in German or French, but plays them with the English timing, because each episode folder also holds a complete English archive. The voices sound choppy.
- **Retro-Version reparieren / Réparer la version rétro** renames the English archive. The game then uses the scenes Telltale timed for the dub back then. Nothing is deleted; switching it off restores everything.

### Season 2: Beyond Time and Space

- About 12,500 recordings and all texts per language, plus about 5,300 scenes that wait for the longer lines.
- Scenes pause like in the remaster: the time curves of their tracks stand still until the line is finished.
- Cut-off dialogs, an overlapping menu and music that repeated its last moment in pauses are fixed.
- **Umlauts:** the Season 2 retro engine shows no characters outside ASCII, whatever the encoding. German umlauts are therefore written out as "ae", "oe", "ue" and "ss".

### Season 3: The Devil's Playhouse

- About 11,200 German recordings and 18,000 texts in 165 dialogs, menus included, plus 4,554 adapted scenes across all five episodes.
- Subtitles show real umlauts, and the menus are German too. Where the DVD version had left menu items in English, its translation of the same term from elsewhere or a common term such as "Einstellungen" is used.
- Everything lives in **one extra file** per episode (`ep30x\Pack\0_0DubPolice.ttarch`). No Steam file is changed. Removing the dub deletes just that file.

## How Season 3 became possible

There is a fan patch for the Season 3 retro mode. It replaces the whole Steam game, program included, with the German 2010 DVD version. Back then, however, Telltale only adapted 5 scenes to the German lines, and all others ran with the English timing. Dub Police goes another way: it leaves the game as it is and makes its existing scenes fit the German lines itself. For that, it first had to understand how this engine stores scenes.

### 1. An archive of its own that comes first

Each episode loads every archive in its `Pack` folder. When a file exists twice, the archive that sorts first wins, just like Telltale's patches (`0_…`) win over the data (`1_…`). Dub Police therefore adds an archive `0_0DubPolice.ttarch` with the German voices, the dialogs with German text and the adapted scenes.

### 2. Subtitles inside the dialog files

In Season 3 the subtitles are not in a text database but right inside the dialog files (`.dlog`). Each line is an entry with its own size field. Dub Police reads these tables completely, replaces the texts and updates every size field. Every dialog then has to read back without errors. The files of episode 302 and some patch files are also obscured; they are revealed with the episode's key.

### 3. The scene format decoded

A scene (choreography, `.chore`) is made of tracks: animations, lines, sounds, cameras, lights, nested scenes. Every track has a **control animation**: channels with keys over time. A time channel says where the track is, a weight channel fades it in and out, and other channels drive position, rotation, visibility, expressions or camera switches. The track's time blocks follow.

The starting point was the format description of the [telltale-explorer](https://github.com/coccofresco/telltale-explorer) project for *Tales of Monkey Island*, the engine generation before. No code from it is in Dub Police. Everything else was worked out and verified on the Season 3 scenes themselves:

- **22 channel types** with their exact sizes: numbers, on/off values, vectors, rotations, texts, references to other scenes, each as a single value or as a list over time.
- **How each key blends** is stored in 2 bits: *step* (the value stays until the next key), *straight line* or *smooth curve*. Camera positions, for example, step at cuts.
- **The memory the game sets aside** for the keys: 4 bytes per channel, the keys rounded up to 4 bytes, and 4 more for every started 16 keys. The formula holds for all 161,406 control animations of the five episodes. If it is wrong, the game crashes while loading.
- **Nested scenes** keep, in their time channel, the share (0 to 1) of the inner scene's length.

The reader is checked strictly: in all **12,454 scenes** of the five episodes it finds exactly as many tracks as the scene's header says, and it writes every scene back **byte for byte identical**.

### 4. Pausing instead of cutting off

When a German line is longer than the English one, the scene needs a pause at that point. Dub Police computes the pauses the same way as for the remaster and then applies them:

- **Every time after a pause moves later:** time blocks, keys of all channels, camera switches, visibility, triggered scenes and the scene's length.
- **Movements stand still:** channels that step or run in straight lines get two hold keys with the value at the start of the pause. Characters, camera and lights stand still until the line has finished.
- **Smooth curves are stretched**, as their exact formula is not known. That is almost only blend weights.
- **The line itself** is stretched to its new length, so voice and subtitle play to the end.
- **Sound and music** fade out and back in during the pause. A held sound would otherwise repeat its last moment.
- **Nested scenes** are adapted first. The outer scene pauses with them and converts their shares to the new length.

Every adapted scene is checked automatically before it goes into the game:

On top of that, every new scene:
- reads back with the same number of tracks,
- has the right memory size,
- and gives every movement, after the shift, the original value at every moment. The only exception is a few rotations with rounding of at most 0.005.

The last bugs were found with videos from the game: cuts and brightness of the original and the new version were laid side by side and compared to the hundredth of a second.

## Remaster improvements

- **Badge on the main menu:** the game's logo is a BC3 texture. Dub Police puts the sticker on it and encodes again only the blocks it touches; the rest of the logo keeps its original bytes. The changed logo lives in a small archive of its own, loaded first through a second description of the menu set. The original stays untouched.
- **Music in dialog pauses:** when a cutscene waited for a longer line, the music used to run on or repeat its last bit. Now it fades out 0.35 s before the pause and back in 0.45 s after it. With **Musik in Dialogpausen ausblenden / Couper la musique pendant les pauses** off, it simply keeps running. This applies to all seasons and to the retro mode.
- **French mouth shapes:** French spelling rules, such as silent endings and nasals, turn the text into mouth shapes. Before, the English movements were only stretched.
- **Gadgets:** talking gadgets have a switch instead of mouth shapes. It now follows the loudness of the new line.
- **Bone lips:** some characters have animations whose keys count frames. Stretched, their mouth jumps. They now get a fitting animation of the same character in the right length.
- **Nested scenes:** the outer scene stores the length of the inner one. It was not updated, so the inner scene played in slow motion and was cut off at the end.

## Known limits

- **Retro Season 3, lips:** the German lips of the DVD version come from a newer engine and do not fit the retro engine. Mouths therefore move as in English.
- **Retro Season 3, facial expressions:** a compressed channel type, probably facial expressions, appears in 4 tracks. Its timing stays unchanged; the other channels of those tracks are moved.
- **Smooth movement curves** are briefly stretched at pauses instead of standing still.
- **Retro Season 2, umlauts:** written out, see above.
- **Played** were the German versions of the remasters with the badge, the repaired Season 1 retro mode, the German Season 2 retro mode and several episodes of the Season 3 retro mode. The French versions are built and checked automatically.

## Installing and updating

1. Download `DubPolice.zip` from the [releases page](https://github.com/MrLuigi938/DubPolice/releases/latest), unpack it and close the game.
2. Start `DubPolice.exe` and choose season and language.
3. For the remasters, click **Stimmen installieren / Installer les voix** as before. For the retro mode, tick **Retro-Version auch vertonen / Doubler aussi la version rétro**, for Season 1 **Retro-Version reparieren / Réparer la version rétro**.

If you already installed an older version, just install the episodes again to get the improvements. There is no need to uninstall first.
